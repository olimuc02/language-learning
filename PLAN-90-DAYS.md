# The 90-Day French Plan — 20 minutes a day

Start date: 2026-08-12 → Target finish: 2026-11-09

## Where you'll end up

After 90 days you should be at a solid **A2 level** ("elementary"). In real terms:

- Handle a whole trip to France without English — airport, hotel, taxi, restaurant, shop, pharmacy
- Hold a 10-minute conversation about yourself, your work, your family, your weekend
- Talk about the past and the future, not just right now
- Read a menu, a sign, a short news headline, a text message
- Understand slow, clear French speech about familiar topics

You will **not** be fluent. Nobody is fluent in 90 days at 20 minutes. But you'll be genuinely functional, and you'll have the foundation that makes year two easy.

---

## The daily 20 minutes

Every lesson follows the same shape so it becomes automatic:

| Minutes | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Warm-up** | Rapid recall of yesterday. No new material. |
| 3–8 | **New words** | 5–8 words or phrases, with a memory hook for each |
| 8–13 | **One grammar idea** | Exactly one rule, explained in plain English, compared to how English does it |
| 13–17 | **Out loud** | You speak. Repeat-after-me, then answer questions in French. |
| 17–20 | **Mini-quiz** | 5 questions. You must get 4/5 to unlock tomorrow. |

**Weekly rhythm:** Days 1–5 = new material. Day 6 = conversation only, no new material. Day 7 = review + weekly checkpoint test.

# PHASE 1 — Ears and Mouth (Days 1–21)

*Goal: stop sounding like an English speaker reading French, and survive a first meeting.*

### Week 1 (Days 1–7) — Sounds and hellos
- **Day 1** — The 4 sounds English doesn't have. Bonjour / Salut / Merci / Au revoir.
- **Day 2** — Silent letters (the single biggest French rule). Ça va ? and answering it.
- **Day 3** — Je suis / Tu es / Il est. Saying your name, where you're from.
- **Day 4** — Numbers 0–20. Age, phone numbers.
- **Day 5** — Le / la / un / une — why every object has a gender, and how to cope.
- **Day 6** — Conversation: a full first meeting, start to finish.
- **Day 7** — Review + Checkpoint 1.

**Week 1 goal:** Introduce yourself and greet someone with a believable accent.
**Measure:** Say a 30-second self-introduction out loud without reading. Score 4/5 or better on Checkpoint 1.

### Week 2 (Days 8–14) — Being and having
- **Day 8** — The full verb *être* (to be) — the most important word in French.
- **Day 9** — The full verb *avoir* (to have). "I'm 40" is "I *have* 40 years."
- **Day 10** — Asking questions three ways (the easy way, the normal way, the fancy way).
- **Day 11** — Saying no: *ne… pas*, the French sandwich.
- **Day 12** — Family and people words.
- **Day 13** — Conversation: talking about your family and Josh, your work.
- **Day 14** — Review + Checkpoint 2.

**Week 2 goal:** Ask and answer basic personal questions.
**Measure:** Answer 10 rapid-fire personal questions in French with no English. Checkpoint 2 ≥ 80%.

### Week 3 (Days 15–21) — Doing things
- **Day 15** — Regular *-er* verbs — one pattern unlocks ~1,000 verbs.
- **Day 16** — *Aller* (to go) + places in a town.
- **Day 17** — *Faire* (to do/make) — used everywhere, including weather.
- **Day 18** — Days, months, telling time.
- **Day 19** — Your daily routine, start to finish.
- **Day 20** — Conversation: describe a typical Tuesday.
- **Day 21** — Review + **Checkpoint 3 (Phase 1 exam)**.

**Week 3 goal:** Describe what you do in a normal day.
**Measure:** Speak for 60 seconds about your day, unscripted. Phase 1 exam ≥ 80%.

# PHASE 2 — Out in the World (Days 22–45)

*Goal: handle real-life transactions. This is the "survive France" phase.*

### Week 4 (Days 22–28) — Food and restaurants
Ordering, the menu, *je voudrais*, quantities, the bill, dietary requests, politeness levels.
- Day 28: Review + Checkpoint 4.

**Goal:** Order a full meal and pay, no English.
**Measure:** Role-play a restaurant scene where I play a rude waiter who talks fast.

### Week 5 (Days 29–35) — Shopping and money
Prices, numbers to 1,000, sizes and colors, "how much", trying things on, returns, markets.
- Day 35: Review + Checkpoint 5.

**Goal:** Buy clothes and groceries and negotiate a market stall.
**Measure:** Handle a 15-exchange shopping role-play.

### Week 6 (Days 36–42) — Getting around
Directions, transport, tickets, hotel check-in, *il y a*, prepositions of place, asking for help.
- Day 42: Review + Checkpoint 6.

**Goal:** Navigate a city you've never been to.
**Measure:** Give someone directions from your hotel to a museum, in French.

### Days 43–45 — **Phase 2 exam + first "French-only day"**
Three days where I speak almost entirely French. English only when you're truly stuck.

**Phase 2 marker:** You should be roughly halfway through A1→A2 here. Expect ~600 words known.

---

# PHASE 3 — The Past and the Future (Days 46–66)

*Goal: stop being trapped in the present tense. This is where you start telling stories.*

### Week 7 (Days 46–52) — The past tense (passé composé)
The single hardest week. Built with *avoir* + past participle, then the 16 verbs that use *être* instead.
- Day 52: Review + Checkpoint 7.

**Goal:** Tell me what you did yesterday and last weekend.

### Week 8 (Days 53–59) — The other past tense (imparfait)
Used for descriptions, habits, "I used to…". Plus when to pick which past tense — explained with a rule that actually works.
- Day 59: Review + **Checkpoint 8 = the A1 exam**.

**Goal:** Describe your childhood and how things used to be.
**Measure:** Checkpoint 8 is a full A1 test — vocabulary, grammar, listening, writing a 60-word text. ≥75% = A1 confirmed.

### Week 9 (Days 60–66) — Pronouns and the future
*le / la / les / lui / leur*, near future (*je vais faire*), simple future, work and business vocabulary.
- Day 66: Review + Checkpoint 9.

**Goal:** Talk about your plans and your business without repeating nouns constantly.

---

# PHASE 4 — Opinions and Real Conversation (Days 67–90)

*Goal: sound like a person, not a phrasebook.*

### Week 10 (Days 67–73) — Opinions and connectors
*je pense que, je trouve que, parce que, mais, donc, quand même*. Agreeing, disagreeing, hedging.
- Day 73: Checkpoint 10.

### Week 11 (Days 74–80) — Health, emergencies, problems
Doctor, pharmacy, pain, police, lost luggage, complaining politely, "there's a problem with…".
- Day 80: Checkpoint 11.

### Week 12 (Days 81–87) — Politeness, conditional, subjunctive intro
*je voudrais / pourriez-vous / il faut que*. Phone calls, emails, formal vs casual register.
- Day 87: Checkpoint 12.

### Week 13 (Days 88–90) — **Final exam, three days**
- **Day 88** — Listening + reading exam.
- **Day 89** — Written exam: 150-word text about your life.
- **Day 90** — **Oral exam:** 15-minute conversation, entirely in French, unscripted, on topics you don't get in advance. I grade it and tell you your honest level.

## How progress gets measured

**Every day:** the 5-question mini-quiz. 4/5 to advance. Below that, tomorrow repeats today.

**Every week:** a Checkpoint with four parts —
1. Vocabulary recall (English → French, cold)
2. Grammar application (fill in / fix the sentence)
3. Listening (I write French, you tell me what it means without a dictionary)
4. Production (you write or say something new)

Scores go in `PROGRESS.md`. Below 75% on a Checkpoint = a repair day before the next week starts.

**Every 3 weeks:** a Phase exam and a "can-do" self-check:

| | Can-do statement | Target day |
|---|---|---|
| ☐ | I can introduce myself and greet people naturally | 21 |
| ☐ | I can order food and buy things without English | 45 |
| ☐ | I can talk about yesterday and last year | 66 |
| ☐ | I can give an opinion and explain why | 80 |
| ☐ | I can hold a 15-minute conversation | 90 |

**Vocabulary count target:** ~150 words by day 21, ~600 by day 45, ~1,100 by day 66, ~1,600 by day 90.
(1,000 words covers about 80% of everyday spoken French. 1,600 is comfortable.)

## If you miss days

Missing days is normal and does not break the plan. Rules:
- **1–3 days missed:** just resume. I'll add extra warm-up.
- **4–10 days missed:** one full review day first, then resume.
- **10+ days missed:** we back up one full week and redo it. It'll go fast.

The plan is 90 *lessons*, not 90 calendar days. Take as long as you need.
